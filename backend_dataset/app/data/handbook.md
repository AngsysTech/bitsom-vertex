# Academic Handbook 2026–27

## 1 Program structure
The B.E. Computer Science program is an eight-semester undergraduate program. A normal academic load is 18–22 units in a semester. Students are expected to complete foundational mathematics, computing core courses, electives, humanities work, and a culminating Practice School or Thesis requirement. The program is designed so that prerequisites form a progression rather than a menu; students should therefore review prerequisite chains before planning later semesters. A student may complete an approved course outside the nominal semester, but all graduation rules still apply.

The academic year is divided into two regular semesters. Registration is the student's responsibility, but the Academic Office publishes the controlling rules and later circulars may amend this handbook. Where a later circular explicitly changes a requirement, the later effective rule applies from the population and date stated in that circular.

## 2 Course categories
Core courses are compulsory courses that establish the computing and mathematical foundation of the degree. Discipline Electives are advanced Computer Science offerings used to build depth in a technical area. Open Electives permit breadth outside the required Computer Science sequence. Humanities courses build communication, social analysis, ethics, and interpretation. Practice School or Thesis is the final experiential component and carries 20 units in the program requirement.

A single course is counted in only one graduation bucket according to the catalog classification active for that course. A course cannot be double-counted across Discipline Electives, Open Electives, Humanities, or Core. Substitutions require written Academic Office approval.

## 3.1 Total units required
A student must earn 142 units to graduate from the B.E. Computer Science program. Units are earned only for courses that receive a passing grade under Section 5.2. Repeated attempts do not create duplicate earned units for the same course.

## 3.2 Graduation requirements
The Core requirement is 78 units. The Discipline Electives requirement is a minimum of 4 courses totaling at least 16 units. The Open Electives requirement is a minimum of 3 courses totaling at least 9 units. The Humanities requirement is 2 courses totaling at least 6 units. The Practice School or Thesis requirement is 20 units. The minimum cumulative grade point average for graduation is 5.0.

These requirements are checked independently. Completing 142 total units does not waive a bucket requirement, a prerequisite, or the minimum CGPA requirement. Students should use the degree audit after every semester because late changes to an elective plan can delay completion even when total units appear sufficient.

## 4.1 Elective buckets
The following course codes count as Discipline Electives: CS F342, CS F363, CS F407, CS F415, CS F429, CS F432, CS F441, CS F464, CS F320, CS F330.

The following course codes count as Open Electives: ECON F211, BIO F201, DES F210, MGTS F231, EEE F245.

Only the codes listed above are recognized in these two elective buckets for this catalog edition. The current catalog remains the authority for title, unit value, semester offering, and skill tags.

## 5.2 Grading and CGPA
Letter grades are converted to grade points as follows: A = 10, A- = 9, B = 8, B- = 7, C = 6, C- = 5, D = 4, E = 2, F = 0. A passing grade for unit credit is D or higher. CGPA is the unit-weighted average of grade points over completed graded courses. An F earns no units and remains relevant to academic standing until the course is cleared according to Academic Office rules.

Internal assessments and end-semester examinations contribute to the final course grade according to the announced course scheme. The September 2026 end-semester circular standardizes the current semester's end-semester examination weight at 40 percent in every course.

## 5.4 Unit overload
Registering above 25 units in a semester requires Associate Dean approval and a CGPA of at least 8.0. Meeting the CGPA threshold does not create an automatic entitlement to overload; the Associate Dean considers prerequisite readiness, timetable feasibility, and prior academic performance. A student without approval should plan at or below 25 units.

## 6.1 Academic standing
A student is on track when the student has earned at least 17 units per completed semester on average. A student below 17 earned units per completed semester on average is classified as at risk for planning purposes. Academic standing is an early-warning measure and does not replace the separate graduation requirements in Section 3.2.

For a student after four completed semesters, the on-track pace benchmark is therefore 68 earned units. Backlogs, failed courses, and incomplete experiential requirements should be reviewed even when a student remains above the pace benchmark.
